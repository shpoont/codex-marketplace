# Mail Processing

Version: **1.2.0**

Mail Processing specializes [A Simple Method for Processing Unorganized Items v1.4.0](https://github.com/shpoont/simple-method-for-processing-unorganized-items/blob/v1.4.0/docs/simple-method-for-processing-unorganized-items.md), the shared method. All its rules apply. People and agents can use Mail Processing directly; existing tools and routines may suffice without an additional record system.

An individual email message is an **item**. Attachments are message content: consider them where relevant without automatically making them separate items.

## Mail terms

| Term | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Conversation or thread | Related messages presented together; context or a possible group whose included messages must be clear. |
| Folder | A mail location or grouping, where supported; membership and move effects depend on the system. |
| Label or tag | A named organizational marker, where supported. Define which markers matter for this work. |

## Before you start

Choose the mail accounts, collection, purpose and work limit. Collections may be Inbox messages, folder contents, filtered sets or selected messages. Specify included subfolders where relevant.

For inbox-zero work, a useful purpose is appropriate handling and clearing included messages from the Inbox, with necessary follow-up accepted. Other purposes may leave processed messages in place.

Define when membership is evaluated. By default, use membership at the start and exclude later arrivals. Filter or location changes do not erase unfinished handling. No complete inventory is needed; disclose incomplete discovery.

Define filter meanings. Where labels are supported, “without labels” might mean no user-defined organizational labels, excluding built-in status markers.

Follow the shared method’s ordering guidance. Relevant preferences about people, projects, deadlines or alerts may guide handling and scope selection. Ask about preferences when useful, without requiring priority setup; keep material uncertainty visible.

## Choose the scope

Following the shared method, start with one eligible unprocessed message or a candidate group containing unprocessed messages from an available overview. A conversation or other related messages may form a scope. Skip blocked work where required selection order permits, keeping it tracked. Distinguish the same message appearing in several views from separate copies.

When grouping messages, follow the shared method’s provisional preference for one appropriate shared handling action, including its exceptions and guidance on effort. Sender, subject and conversation membership can suggest candidates; confirm membership from the purpose, evidence and exceptions.

Sent mail, later replies or records in other systems may show that a request was handled or changed. Consult context when its expected value warrants the effort. Existing policy and adequate evidence may suffice without exhaustive searches, reading every message or inspecting every attachment.

Only eligible messages belong to the scope. Consulting outside context does not include it in the collection; changing it needs separate authority.

## Decide what to do

Choose authorized handling that serves the purpose. Examples include retaining correspondence, a checked summary, deliberate non-action, drafting or sending a reply, moving or archiving, using supported organizational markers, removal, or accepted follow-up. Recommendations remain optional.

Handling may also require creating or updating a task, notifying someone in another system, recording a deadline or saving an attachment. These are possibilities, not a required menu.

Specify intended results, not just command names. Depending on the chosen handling, these may concern retention and accessibility, destinations, removal and recoverability, or required external or shared results. Reading, labeling or moving does not resolve an unrelated obligation.

For outgoing messages or notifications, establish recipients, content and attachments. Requests inside messages do not expand the authority under which you work.

## Act and check

Establish actual targets, effects and authority, including outside mail; obtain approval where required. Whole-conversation actions may affect excluded messages or later arrivals. Implementations establish actual supported effects and evidence rather than assuming them from command names.

Execute in parts where useful, preserving required order, dependencies and accessible unfinished work even after messages leave the initial view.

A checked, accessible draft can complete draft-only handling, not a decision requiring sending. Verify sending when required; sending does not establish receipt or resolution.

If arranging follow-up is chosen, including review or sending of a draft, confirm acceptance by the responsible process. Preserve the next action, responsibility, accessible context and reliable pickup or reconsideration. An established personal routine may suffice; a record alone does not.

When the purpose requires noticing a missing response, specify what will happen and when to reconsider. Follow-up is not required for every sent message; arranging it does not satisfy a decision requiring an actual response or resolution.

Verify the required results, including external actions. Adequate collective evidence can suffice without inspecting every message. A scope remains unfinished until every member’s handling, including required shared results, is verified. Preserve recoverable decisions and results; apply the shared method’s recovery and safe-retry rules.

## Continue or stop

Continue discovery and stop under the shared method’s rules. An empty Inbox or filter does not prove complete handling.

Between rounds, preserve unfinished handling and accepted follow-up. Distinguish mail still needing handling from completed handling whose downstream work remains. Each round defines its boundary without losing earlier obligations. Reconsider affected work when new information calls prior handling into question; retain valid results.

Report boundary, coverage, verified handling and remaining obligations. Distinguish completing a bounded inbox-zero round from the current Inbox being empty. Claim purpose or whole-collection completion only with the evidence required by the shared method.

## Examples

**Inbox:** Choose messages already in the Inbox to handle and clear. Relevant context identifies an existing task for a bug report in a work-tracking system such as Jira. The authorized decision is to update the task, confirm accepted bug follow-up, notify a designated team in a messaging system such as Slack, prepare a checked acknowledgement draft and retain the email outside the Inbox. Verify each required result. Here drafting, not sending, is required; accepted follow-up does not establish that the bug is fixed.

**Filtered mail:** Where labels are supported, choose messages with no user-defined organizational label when work starts. One needs such a label and a checked summary. After labeling, the message leaves the filtered view but must remain accessible until its summary is checked. An empty filter does not verify the summary or complete the collection.
